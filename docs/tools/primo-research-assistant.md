## Key claims

### [A March 2025 case study of Polish public-university libraries found limited adoption of Primo Research Assistant despite technical availability; non-adopters reported functional limitations and gaps in awareness of implementation capabilities.](../claims/assistant-011.md)

**Status:** provisional

- **supports** — [Case Study of the Implementation of AI Primo Research Assistant (Beta Version) in Academic Libraries in Poland](../evidence/kotula-2026.md) (peer-reviewed-study); locator: Purpose of the Study and Methods, PDF pp. 2-3; non-adopter results and Discussion, pp. 11-13. The authors identified 19 Primo VE libraries among 65 Polish public-university libraries, of which nine had enabled the beta assistant. They report six adopter and eight non-adopter survey responses, with surveys open March 25-31, 2025. Six of eight non-adopters intended to implement it; two had not considered it. Reported reasons for non-adoption included poor functionality, unsatisfactory humanities results, and awaiting a better version. Two respondents mistakenly believed they lacked the necessary discovery infrastructure. These are institutional reports, not verified performance failures or causal estimates of adoption barriers; the small sample limits generalization beyond this setting and period.

### [A small Polish library-director survey reported perceived search and information-service benefits from Primo Research Assistant alongside trust and metadata concerns, with limited evaluation data and doubts about meeting users' information needs; it does not establish measured workload savings or retrieval quality.](../claims/assistant-012.md)

**Status:** provisional

- **supports** — [Case Study of the Implementation of AI Primo Research Assistant (Beta Version) in Academic Libraries in Poland](../evidence/kotula-2026.md) (peer-reviewed-study); locator: Adopter survey results, PDF pp. 8-11, Figures 3-5; Discussion, pp. 11 and 14; Appendix A, pp. 16-17. Of the five responses reflected in the adopter evaluation items, 60% lacked information about user evaluations and 80% lacked information about staff evaluations. Three responses identified information-service support and three identified easier retrieval; one selected faster work. Three of five said the assistant could not meet users' information needs. Open-ended concerns included trust, better metadata, local metadata integration, and usage statistics. Figure 5 shows overall ratings of 5, 7, 8, 8, and 9 on a 1-10 scale. The abstract and discussion emphasize workload reduction, but Figure 4 shows zero selections for that item; this internal discrepancy is retained rather than resolved. Director reports are not direct user-satisfaction measurements, timed comparisons, or relevance/recall benchmarks. The authors report six adopter responses overall, so item-level denominators should not be assumed to be six.
