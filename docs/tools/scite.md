### [An exploratory study found favourable literature-review output features for paid Scite on one English/Spanish e-reading topic, without establishing Scite Assistant's factual accuracy or source relevance.](../claims/assistant-037.md)

**Status:** provisional

- **supports** — [AI literature review systems: an analysis of performance, affordances, and outputs for a complex topic in the social sciences](../evidence/moulaison-sandy-2025.md) (peer-reviewed-study); locator: Method, PDF pp. 4-5 (1247-1248); Tables 1-2, p. 5 (1248); observations, p. 6; limitations, p. 7. Four paid tools (Scite, Jenni.ai, OpenRead, Wisio) were piloted and then demonstrated with a shared initial 500-1000-word literature-review prompt, interactively refined, and collaboratively coded. Scite produced more than 500 words, permitted date selection and methodology summaries, and supplied ten primary-source references, links, APA formatting, and an export with references. It could not restrict sources to peer-reviewed items or by language, although output could be generated in Spanish. Table 2 marks no reference hallucinations for any of the four systems. The paper calls the product Scite and does not identify Assistant by name, a model/version, testing date, or repeated-run count. It explicitly reserves text accuracy and source relevance for future work. The positive affordance findings are not an answer-accuracy or completeness benchmark.

### [In a four-topic cardiology comparison, Scite Assistant had the lowest median proportions of relevant and expert-defined key references among retrieved articles; these precision measures do not estimate exhaustive recall.](../claims/assistant-036.md)

**Status:** supported

- **supports** — [Comparative evaluation of artificial intelligence–assisted literature search tools for identifying clinically meaningful evidence in cardiology](../evidence/di-febo-2026.md) (peer-reviewed-study); locator: Methods and metric definitions, PDF pp. 3-4; Results and Table 2, pp. 5-6; Figure 4, p. 7; limitations, p. 9. Free-tier Scite Assistant supplemented its citation database with the open web. Four prompts across four CRT topics were each submitted once, collecting up to ten ranked references per condition (113 eligible Scite retrieval records). Three blinded experts rated a pooled tool/SLR corpus; mean relevance above 2.5 on a 0-3 scale defined clinically relevant items. Scite medians were 20% relevant articles (IQR 17-31%) and 10% key references (IQR 0-20%); ChatGPT-5 medians were 90% and 60%. Methods explicitly divides both metrics by retrieved articles. The 10% is not the proportion of all key references recovered. One subdomain, free tiers, one run per prompt, corpus incorporation, and different intended tool functions limit generalization. Generated-answer factuality was not measured.
