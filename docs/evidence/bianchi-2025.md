# Data Extractions Using a Large Language Model (Elicit) and Human Reviewers in Randomized Controlled Trials: A Systematic Comparison

**Source:** 2025; peer-reviewed study

## Bibliographic information

- **Authors:** Joleen Bianchi, Julian Hirt, Magdalena Vogt, Janine Vetsch
- **Year:** 2025
- **DOI:** 10.1002/cesm.70033
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1002/cesm.70033
- **Publication status:** Published in Cochrane Evidence Synthesis and Methods 3:e70033
- **Source category:** peer-reviewed-study
- **Record type:** journal-article
- **Notes:** Compared Elicit with checked human extractions for seven fields across 20 randomized trials (140 field-level comparisons). Elicit provided more correct information in 29.3%, equivalent information in 20.7%, incomplete information in 45.7%, and incorrect information in 4.3%. Completeness was weakest for intervention descriptions and effects. The convenience sample was small, the reference summaries were in German, and five papers contained relevant information in visual elements Elicit could not extract.
